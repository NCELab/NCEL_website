Research interns

Kelly Wang intern Columbia University / New York State Psychiatric Institute

Kelly is a second -year M.A. student in the Clinical Psychology program at Teachers College, Columbia University. Her work in the NCEL lab focuses on data management and EEG preprocessing. She is deeply passionate about leveraging neuroimaging techniques (EEG, fMRI) within experimental paradigms to investigate how adverse or traumatic experiences shape cognitive functioning and emotional processing. Grounded in this perspective, she aims to advance research that integrates biological, psychological, and experiential levels of analysis to inform more culturally responsive, accessible, and neuroscience-informed mental health interventions, with the long-term goal of pursuing a PhD in Clinical Psychology.
Jumi Park intern Columbia University / New York State Psychiatric Institute

Jumi is a second-year M.A. student in the Clinical Psychology program at Teachers College. Her research interests focus on understanding the underlying mechanisms of emotion and exploring how artificial intelligence and technology can be used to increase access to psychotherapy.
Max Sklar intern Columbia University / New York State Psychiatric Institute

Max is a second -year master’s student in the Clinical Psychology program at Teachers College with a concentration in Forensic Psychology. Their research interests include wartime sexual violence through an intersectional lens, conflict resolution in Israel/Palestine and other conflict-affected regions, and reducing sexual violence within carceral systems, with a particular focus on its disproportionate impact on queer incarcerated individuals.
Sanya Vadhavkar Intern Columbia University / New York State Psychiatric Institute

Sanya is a second-year M.A. student in Clinical Psychology at Teachers College, Columbia University. She is interested in contributing to more holistic approaches to healthcare and hopes to work at the intersection of neuroscience and psychology. As she continues exploring her research interests, she is particularly drawn to understanding how brain and behavioral science can inform more integrated approaches to mental health and well-being.
Morgan Arma Intern Columbia University / New York State Psychiatric Institute

Morgan is a graduate student at Teachers College, Columbia University, where she is pursuing a master’s degree in psychology. Her academic and research interests include clinical psychology, trauma and resilience, suicide prevention, substance use treatment, and the ways social and interpersonal factors shape mental health. She is especially interested in working with individuals and communities affected by adversity, including veterans, adolescents, and people navigating anxiety, depression, or substance use concerns. In the future, Morgan hopes to pursue a doctoral-level degree in psychology and continue building her clinical and research skills as she works toward becoming a clinical psychologist
