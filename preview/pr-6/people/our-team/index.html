## Our Team ### Research Coordinator
Daniel Craft Research Coordinator Fairleigh Dickinson University/ Columbia University / New York State Psychiatric Institute

Daniel is a Clinical Psychology PhD student at Fairleigh Dickinson University whose work focuses on trauma, psychopathology, affective processes, and cognitive-emotional regulation. For the past six years, he has been a member of the NeuroCognition and Emotion Lab, where he currently serves as the Research Coordinator. In this role, he has managed large-scale research projects examining PTSD, intergenerational trauma, cognition, and emotional functioning, including studies involving World Trade Center responder families. He has contributed to multiple peer-reviewed publications and presentations at national and international conferences, including the Association for Psychological Science and the International Society for Traumatic Stress Studies. His broader focus is on the goal of integrating rigorous empirical research with evidence-based clinical practice.
### Research Assistants
Ruixuan Zhao Research Assistant Columbia University / New York State Psychiatric Institute

Ruixuan Zhao received her M.A. in Quantitative Methods in the Social Sciences from Columbia University with a concentration in Data Science and her B.A. in Psychology from Barnard College. As a research assistant at the NeuroCognition and Emotion Lab, she has contributed to projects examining trauma, cognition, and mental health outcomes. Her research interests focus on trauma and human cognition, particularly the neural mechanisms underlying memory, emotion, and cognitive control, with an interest in applying neuroimaging and computational approaches to better understand mental health and behavior.
### Interns
Kelly Wang intern Columbia University / New York State Psychiatric Institute

Kelly is a second -year M.A. student in the Clinical Psychology program at Teachers College, Columbia University. Her work in the NCEL lab focuses on data management and EEG preprocessing. She is deeply passionate about leveraging neuroimaging techniques (EEG, fMRI) within experimental paradigms to investigate how adverse or traumatic experiences shape cognitive functioning and emotional processing. Grounded in this perspective, she aims to advance research that integrates biological, psychological, and experiential levels of analysis to inform more culturally responsive, accessible, and neuroscience-informed mental health interventions, with the long-term goal of pursuing a PhD in Clinical Psychology.
