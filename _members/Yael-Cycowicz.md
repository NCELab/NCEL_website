Dr. Cycowicz is a cognitive neuroscientist whose research examines how brain systems support cognition, emotion, and behavior across development and the lifespan. She combines neurophysiological methods, including MEG, EEG, and fMRI, with innovative experimental paradigms and computational approaches to investigate the neural mechanisms underlying attention, memory, decision-making, and emotional regulation.

Her research spans neuroplasticity and the development of cognitive functions from childhood through older adulthood, as well as how cognition and emotion are altered by trauma and psychiatric disorders, including attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), anxiety, depression, and substance use disorders. Her current work focuses particularly on trauma, memory, resilience, and the intergenerational transmission of trauma, examining how experiences can shape individuals and families across time.

Across these areas, Dr. Cycowicz seeks to bridge basic neuroscience and clinical science, connecting neural mechanisms with the lived experiences of individuals and families. Her work is driven by the broader question of how experiences become embedded in memory, behavior, and the brain, and how those effects may persist, change, or be transformed across the lifespan and across generations.
