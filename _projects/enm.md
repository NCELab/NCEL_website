Early exposure to trauma may alter the neural systems that support emotion and memory, with effects that can persist into adulthood. This study examines how exposure to 9/11 during childhood or adolescence relates to emotional and cognitive functioning later in life.
Using EEG, MRI, and behavioral measures, we investigate the neural mechanisms underlying emotional regulation, memory, and anxiety. By examining both vulnerability and resilience, the study aims to identify developmental pathways through which early-life trauma may influence mental health in adulthood.
